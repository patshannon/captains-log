# Captain's Log — 2026-06-11
Cinema work dominated the day across two private projects, and it was mostly satisfying, detail-oriented work.

The main effort was wiring up a "contenus connexes" carousel on cinema content pages — the kind of related-content surface that sounds simple until you're actually doing it. Six files touched on the frontend side, which tracks: you've got the component itself, whatever data-fetching or prop plumbing is needed, and invariably some layout or styling considerations. Speaking of which, I also fixed a nagging visual artifact where placeholder poster images were getting their corners clipped incorrectly at rounded boundaries. It's one of those small things that nobody explicitly notices when it's right, but everybody notices when it's wrong. The fix was in two files — almost certainly `overflow: hidden` on the right container, or ensuring border-radius is applied consistently with a clip path.

On the API side in a second private project, the related work was mapping the "contenus connexes" data structure and resolving related film references within cinema compilations. This is the backend complement to the frontend carousel — making sure the data model actually surfaces the right relationships so the UI has something coherent to render.

Merged a quick but important fix in a third project: Twitter card meta tags were using the wrong attribute. Twitter's `<meta>` tags require `name` rather than `property` (that's the OG/Facebook convention), and getting them mixed up means your cards silently fail to render. Easy one-liner fix, but these are the kinds of silent failures that are genuinely hard to notice in testing if you're not actively checking the Twitter Card Validator.

The most substantive standalone work was in a fourth project: hardening a download processing pipeline against CDN throttling. The commit adds exponential backoff when the CDN rate-limits requests, gracefully skips downloads that fail rather than blowing up the entire job, and surfaces per-stage error state into the state document so failures are observable after the fact. All three of those are important independently, but together they turn what was presumably a brittle all-or-nothing process into something resilient enough to run in production without babysitting.
