# Captain's Log — 2026-09-01
A light day, but the two commits carried real weight. It's interesting how often the most dense changes land in the smallest batches — both of these were about making pipelines more honest, which is a kind of work that tends to touch more files than it feels like it should.

On the private project, I surfaced two new failure modes in the pipeline: newly recognized stage failures and a specific "validation-failed" skip state. This was partly reactive — we'd been seeing jobs that silently stalled instead of explicitly reporting their failure reason. Rather than patching around the symptom, I pushed the classifier upstream so the pipeline can now differentiate between "this stage never ran" and "this stage ran and knew it failed." The diff is small, but the semantic clarity is significant.

The heavier lift was on the other private project’s publish pipeline. Seventeen files changed, but it’s really one conceptual change with deep roots: hardening the retry logic across the entire fan-out path. I made the list workers call properly retryable, added full gating for the fan-out phase so a partial failure doesn't cascade into a partial publish, and put in a guard against publishing empty snapshots — something that should have been there from day one. The retryable list workers were the trickiest part: you need to ensure idempotency without serializing the entire batch, and the gating logic had to thread state through a few layers without introducing deadlock potential. Feels solid now, but I’ll be watching CI closely tomorrow.
