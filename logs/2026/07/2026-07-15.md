# Captain's Log — 2026-07-15
Heavy day across two projects, with the bulk of the action landing on the feed and auth layers of private-project-2 before capping off a bigger content infrastructure push on private-project-1.

The private-project-2 work centered on getting a replay-backed feed into shape for the live stage — a 49-file PR that apparently required a lot of cleanup before it was merge-worthy. The interesting stuff is in the details: karaoke highlights now track the actual voice timing in published replays rather than some approximated schedule, beat advancement is keyed to the clip's real end rather than wall-clock time (a subtle but meaningful distinction — wall-clock drift kills synchronization in ways that are hard to debug later), and replay tempo is restored to natural 1x speed. All of this points to a system where playback fidelity matters and the earlier implementation was taking too many shortcuts.

The word alignment work is worth calling out separately. Rebasing the backend's spoken-token alignment onto caption words — twice, across two commits — suggests the first pass didn't fully account for how the backend structures its output in the latest model cycle. That kind of impedance mismatch between a backend's token segmentation and a frontend's caption representation is always fiddlier than it looks on paper.

On the auth side, the stale session self-healing fix is the kind of thing you punt on until you can't. Stale `__session` tokens on action paths will silently fail for users in ways that look like application bugs rather than auth issues. Having the client detect and recover from that state is the right call. The iOS zoom fix — bumping form inputs to 16px — is one of those permanently on the checklist items that still somehow escapes every new project.

Over on private-project-1, a substantial infrastructure PR landed: a full compilation pipeline for Cinema content pages, a dedicated API endpoint, and an Algolia index wired up for search. Plus rights expiry handling, which is the kind of business logic that's easy to underestimate — expiry states touch display, access control, and indexing all at once. Getting that right on the way in is much easier than retrofitting it.

Solid output. Both PRs merged clean.
