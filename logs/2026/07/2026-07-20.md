# Captain's Log — 2026-07-20
Sprawling day across five projects — the kind where you're context-switching every hour but somehow land meaningful things in each repo.

The heaviest lift was in private-project-1, which absorbed the majority of commits and PRs. Two features shipped: a shareable Lawn Score card and a lawn timeline showing score trends alongside a treatment log. Both are the kind of user-facing work that makes a product feel real — not just diagnostic output, but a record and a story. The timeline PR involved a merge from main mid-flight, which is always a small tax on focus, but nothing dramatic. Beyond those features, I spent time on marketing and compliance hygiene: correctly disclosing the geocoding, weather, and telemetry providers, fixing an inaccurate password claim, and opening a PR for a proper privacy page linked from both the landing page and signup flow. This stuff isn't glamorous but it matters — shipping without it is technical debt of a different kind. Also hid a dev-only weather readout from production, which should have happened earlier. And there's a subtle-but-important bug fix in the budget guard: failed diagnoses weren't persisting their AI spend, meaning the daily cost cap could be silently bypassed. That's the kind of off-by-one logic that bleeds money quietly.

private-project-5 had its own big moment — the profile system landed. Public and private profile pages at `/@username` handles, with editing, avatar support, and fallbacks. That's a meaningful feature surface and the PR touched 28 files, so the review findings on the follow-up commit were expected: server-side handle validation, redirect behavior on rename, onboarding unblocking, and avatar edge cases. All addressed in a single tidy patch. Good pattern — ship the feature, address review notes atomically rather than dragging them across multiple PRs.

private-project-2 and private-project-4 got parallel attention around cinema search facet alignment — mapping filter fields to what the client actually sends. The kind of work that looks trivial in a diff but is load-bearing: wrong field names mean silent mismatches and queries that return nothing useful.

Finally, private-project-3 got a reusable Firebase QR redirect component — 11 files for something ostensibly small suggests it's a properly abstracted, generalizable piece rather than a one-off hack. Good investment if QR flows appear in multiple places.

Long day, but clean endings on most threads.
