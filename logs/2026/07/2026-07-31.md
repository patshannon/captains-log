# Captain's Log — 2026-07-31
A heavy day across three workstreams, and all of them moved meaningfully.

The bulk of the private-project-1 effort was on cinema cards — the kind of UI polish work that sounds trivial until you're in the middle of reconciling four different card sizes, two aspect ratios, and a Figma spec that assumed everything is the same width. The shift to 16:9 stills on wide cinema cards is the right call; the old approach was pulling portrait-oriented thumbnails into landscape slots and hoping nobody noticed. Alongside that, I standardized responsive cinema card layouts and header heights, which had drifted enough to be visually inconsistent depending on viewport. A few smaller things rounded out the branch: the dark filter styles now match Figma, taxonomy tab layout got a fix, and collection page URLs were canonicalized to `/collections/:slug/:id` — a long-overdue cleanup that makes the routing predictable. Disabled a metadata component on cinema pages temporarily while a related ticket clears; sometimes the right call is to ship clean and re-enable later rather than block on a dependency.

Private-project-3 was the data layer counterpart to some of that card work — carrying the 16:9 still into the cinema film card schema, adding an `isPartner` field for editorial consistency, and making description and ranking available on collection cards. Small surface area, but it unblocks the UI from having to fake or omit that data.

Private-project-2 was the real marathon. Five PRs merged, touching profile tabs, social images, and feed filtering. The profile tab work landed real Scenarios and Replays grids with studio entry points — replacing placeholder UI with actual content grids backed by live data. Alongside that, OG social images now pull from actual scenario/replay content and get served through a cached route, which should meaningfully improve link previews. I also fixed an OG URL resolution bug where images were resolving to a protected deployment host rather than the public one — easy miss, surprisingly impactful.

On the feed side, the big one was adopting the canonical CMS tag vocabulary with server-side filtering, a community-first content overlay, and a languages filter group with cross-conditioned facet counts. Getting facet counts to update correctly when multiple filter groups interact is always fiddly — you have to count against the complement of the current group's selection, not the full filtered set. The "For You" pill removal was a clean consequence of the onboarding auto-apply landing: the explicit affordance became redundant, so it went away. That's a good kind of deletion.
