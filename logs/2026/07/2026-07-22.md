# Captain's Log — 2026-07-22
A satisfying day — shipped one meaningful feature, hunted down a gnarly SVG bug, and kept two other workstreams moving forward.

The centerpiece was landing the takes-axis swipe in the first private project's feed. The idea is straightforward enough: if a scenario has multiple recordings, you can now swipe horizontally to move between them, orthogonal to whatever vertical navigation was already there. Getting the gesture recognizer, the data model, and the UI to all agree on what "sideways" means without stomping on the existing scroll behavior is the fiddly part of this kind of two-axis navigation. Seventeen files changed tells the story — it touches the feed view, the underlying data layer, and probably a fair amount of plumbing in between. PR #33 opened and merged same day, which is always satisfying.

Right before that merge, I fixed a particularly satisfying class of bug. The instructions overlay has two arrows — vertical and horizontal — drawn as SVG paths. The horizontal arrow's right arrowhead was malformed: one of the two tip strokes went *forward* past the arrowhead tip instead of doubling back toward the shaft. The shape was visibly kinked, and somewhere along the way someone had papered over it with a translate/scale hack to re-center the lopsided result. Pat caught it in a field shot. The fix was to mirror the vertical arrow's construction exactly — shaft plus two backward strokes per tip — and pull out the compensating transform entirely. Clean geometry, no band-aids.

Over on the second private project, the cinema feature branch absorbed a main-branch sync (16 files, so main has been moving), and separately I hardened favorites list operations — likely guarding against concurrent mutations or edge cases around empty/nil state. That kind of defensive work doesn't feel glamorous but it prevents the subtle data-corruption bugs that are miserable to diagnose later.

Finally, a staging-to-production PR went up for the third private project, which suggests that workstream is in a release-gate moment rather than active development today.

Good spread across the board.
