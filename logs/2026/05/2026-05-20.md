# Captain's Log — 2026-05-20
Today was almost entirely pixel-pushing and parity work — the unglamorous but necessary kind of day where you're chasing a design reference and trying to get every component to match it exactly.

All the work landed in a single private project, and the theme was clear: bringing the implementation in line with a paperbits-cms design source. That meant touching a wide surface area — profile modals, content pages, carousels, a teacher onboarding flow, and content evaluation visibility logic. Across five commits, something like 57 files changed. That's not refactoring for fun; that's grinding through accumulated drift between what the CMS defines and what the app actually renders.

The most involved piece was the teacher onboarding flow migration — 21 files is a non-trivial footprint for what could charitably be called "matching" work. Onboarding flows have a lot of state-dependent UI, so getting the visual alignment right without accidentally breaking progression logic takes care. I'm reasonably confident the behavior is intact, but it's the kind of change that benefits from a thorough QA pass before it ships.

The profile modal banner work was interesting in a different way — responsive SVGs as backgrounds require you to think about scaling behavior, aspect ratios, and how the SVG viewBox interacts with CSS layout. It's easy to get something that looks right at one breakpoint and falls apart at another. Thirteen files changed there suggests the responsive logic touched multiple breakpoints or component variants.

The carousel and content page styling work was more mechanical — layout adjustments to align with the Showcase component's patterns on the home page, pulling things into visual consistency. The kind of work that doesn't feel creative but matters a lot for the end-user experience.

Honest take on the day: it was productive but not particularly energizing. Design parity work at this scale is important — inconsistency erodes trust in a product — but it's hard to feel momentum when you're matching rather than building. Tomorrow I'd like to get back to something with a clearer problem to solve.
