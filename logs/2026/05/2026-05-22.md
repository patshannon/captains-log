# Captain's Log — 2026-05-22
Today was essentially a long closing sprint on a parity audit I've been running to align a content-managed frontend with its Paperbits-generated production baseline. The work touched French-language marketing and utility pages — contact, FAQ, a discovery landing page, legal URLs — and the goal throughout was to close the gap between what the CMS was rendering and what Paperbits had been producing.

The most substantial commit was the `/decouvrir` marketing landing page, which involved 26 files and also consolidated a scattered set of legal URL references into a unified pattern. That kind of URL normalization sounds boring but it's exactly the type of debt that causes subtle breakage downstream when routes change. Getting it done as part of a parity migration rather than as its own ticket was the right call.

From there it was methodical: migrate FAQ sections 3–7, align the contact page, polish banner and typography against the production reference. The FAQ work in particular requires discipline — it's easy to let "close enough" creep in when you're iterating on structured content, and close enough tends to not survive a real diff.

The audit itself is what I'm most satisfied wrapping up. Ten-plus commits resolving GAP items one by one, some as genuine fixes (GAP-2 required cross-repo verification to confirm a URL was safe to change), some as deliberate N/A calls where the production feature simply doesn't apply anymore — `/jeu`, the newsletter flow, three legacy deep-link parameters. Knowing when to close something as N/A rather than chasing it is a real judgment call, and I think the decisions here were sound.

GAP-6 was the last live fix: hiding content evaluations on toolkit pages. Two files, small surface area, but it had been surfaced during the batched verification pass of 17 audit items and needed to ship with the rest.

The final commit condensed everything into a single closure note across 16 files — a documentation pass to make sure the audit trail is readable by anyone picking this up later. Not glamorous, but a parity audit that doesn't leave a clear record of what was checked and why is only half the work.
