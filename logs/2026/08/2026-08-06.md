# Captain's Log — 2026-08-06
Today was a heavy day across three different projects, with the bulk of the work centered on a cinema feature branch I’ve been building out for the past few weeks. The goal is to introduce a dedicated cinema section on the site, pulling in pedagogical content, subject headings, and advisory notices that were previously only available on the main film pages. The commits tell a story of careful integration: renaming "Ressources" to "Catalogue" in the nav, gating pedagogical documents behind user type (hiding them entirely for anonymous visitors), and fixing a layout quirk where the pedagogical section was pinned to the bottom of the column instead of staying in flow. I also had to hold collection-page queries until the collection scope resolved—a race condition that was causing flickering renders. The most satisfying part was merging the RVM subject headings into the cinema Sujets tab and wiring up the "Fiche du film" (film summary) to appear in the pedagogical downloads area. That required a coordinated effort across two repos: private-project-1 for the frontend and private-project-2 for the API exposure.

On the API side, I exposed the cinema age restriction to Algolia indexing and to the related-film cards, renamed `seriesGuide` to `generalGuide` (a more accurate name for the field), and surfaced the `Avertissement pédagogique` (pedagogical advisory) to cinema pages so the tooltip can show a warning when appropriate. These are the kind of plumbing changes that are invisible to users but essential for the feature to work.

A quick detour into private-project-3 fixed a frustrating bug: PDF downloads were opening `about:blank` because the S3 upload for attachments was being served without a `Content-Length` header. The root cause was a missing content-length in the pre-signed upload request, which I patched by explicitly setting it. That PR got merged and opened—same fix, one commit.

I also squeezed in a push to the captains-log repo to keep this log in sync. A productive day, even if it felt like threading a lot of needles at once.
