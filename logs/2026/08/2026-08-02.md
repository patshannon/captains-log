# Captain's Log — 2026-08-02
Spent the day doing something that doesn't get nearly enough credit: editing. Specifically, reworking my resume site to tell a more coherent story.

The core shift was reframing the portfolio around platform modernization as a through-line. A lot of the work I've done over the past few years has involved taking aging systems — legacy stacks, outdated architectures, underserved content platforms — and dragging them into something more maintainable and scalable. That theme was buried in the previous version. Now it's the organizing principle.

The biggest concrete addition was surfacing a Télé-Québec migration prominently in the selected work section. It's a strong example of exactly that kind of engagement: a meaningful, technically complex project with a real institution, and it wasn't getting the visibility it deserved on the page. Alongside that, I merged a PR adding an "En Classe" project to the lineup, rounding out the picture of that kind of platform work.

On the visual side, I tightened up the desktop layout for project cards. Nothing dramatic — mostly spacing and alignment — but the kind of detail that makes the difference between a page that looks polished and one that just looks functional. Those micro-fixes are easy to deprioritize, but they compound.

It's a bit odd spending engineering time on a resume, but honestly I think of it as a design and communication problem as much as anything else. The constraint is interesting: how do you represent years of nuanced technical work in a format that someone might scan in thirty seconds? The answer, I've come to believe, is ruthless thematic focus. Not "here's everything I've done," but "here's what I'm good at and why it matters." Today felt like a meaningful step toward that.
